Ergonomics in front of the screen

🎯 Goal: To learn how to correctly set up our workstation and work in front of a computer without straining our body.

5.1 Risks from prolonged computer use

Long hours of computer work can lead to:

  • Musculoskeletal problems (neck, shoulder, back, arm pain).
  • Tendinitis or carpal tunnel syndrome.
  • Eye strain, blurred vision, headaches.
  • Mental fatigue, irritability and stress.
  • Reduced concentration and productivity.

All of these can be prevented with proper ergonomics.

5.2 Organising the workstation

✅ Screen position

  • The top of the screen should be at eye level.
  • If it is too low → neck pain.
  • If it is too high → shoulder strain.
  • Eye-to-screen distance: about 50–70 cm (roughly an arm’s length).
  • Avoid light reflections on the screen.
  • Ideally, natural light comes from the side – not from in front or behind.

✅ Chair & body position

  • The chair should support the lower back (lumbar support).
  • Feet flat on the floor or on a footrest.
  • Thighs parallel to the floor, knees slightly bent.
  • Shoulders relaxed, elbows close to the body.
  • Wrist aligned with the forearm.

✅ Keyboard & mouse

  • Placed at the same level, close to the body.
  • The mouse should not be too far or too far to the side.
  • A wrist rest or ergonomic mouse pad can help.

5.3 Working time – breaks

Continuous work in front of a screen tires the body and the eyes.

🕑 20-20-20 rule

Every 20 minutes, look at something 6 metres (20 feet) away for 20 seconds. This relaxes the eyes.

🧘‍♀️ Short breaks

  • Every hour, stand up for 2–3 minutes.
  • Do a few neck and shoulder stretches.
  • Stretch your arms, stretch your body or walk a little.

5.4 Lighting and environment

  • The room should be well lit but not glaring.
  • Natural lighting is preferred, without harsh shadows.
  • Ventilate the office regularly.
  • Temperature should be around 20–24°C.

5.5 Practical tips

  • Set screen brightness correctly (not too bright).
  • Clean the screen regularly with an appropriate cloth.
  • Avoid working in twisted or hunched postures.
  • Use a headset instead of holding the phone between shoulder and head.
  • Alternate between sitting and standing where possible.

Example

Eleni worked 8 hours a day hunched over the screen. After a few months, she had constant headaches and low back pain. With small changes (raising the screen, supporting her lower back and taking hourly breaks) the symptoms disappeared.

🟢 “Ergonomics is not a luxury – it is prevention. Our body is the most valuable ‘tool’ at work.”
